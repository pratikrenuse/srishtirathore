Root canal retreatment in New Chandigarh

A tooth that has already had a root canal can become painful or infected again, sometimes years later. Retreatment means reopening the tooth, removing the old filling material, cleaning the canals again and sealing them properly. Managing these cases is a core part of endodontic specialist training.

Why an earlier root canal may fail

  • A canal was narrow, curved or hidden and was not fully cleaned the first time.
  • The crown or filling was delayed, or has leaked, and bacteria re-entered the tooth.
  • New decay has formed under an old crown.
  • The tooth has cracked.
  • A piece of instrument separated inside the canal during earlier treatment.

How it is done

  1. Assessment

    X-rays, and a CBCT scan where needed, show the old root filling and any untreated canals.

  2. Reopening the tooth

    The tooth is numbed and opened. Old filling material is removed from the canals.

  3. Finding what was missed

    The canals are explored, cleaned and disinfected again along their full length.

  4. Resealing

    The canals are sealed again. Bioceramic materials are used where they suit the case.

  5. New restoration

    The tooth gets a new, well sealed filling or crown so the problem does not return.

Aftercare

  • Expect some soreness for a few days, as after a first root canal.
  • Healing of the bone around the root is checked with follow up X-rays over the coming months.
  • Have the final crown or restoration placed on time. Delay is a common cause of reinfection.

Common questions

My root canal was done years ago. Can it still fail?

Yes. A treated tooth can be reinfected if the seal breaks down, new decay forms, or a canal was left untreated. Pain, swelling or a gum boil near an old root canal should be checked.

Is retreatment always possible?

Not always. It depends on how much sound tooth remains, the shape of the roots and whether there is a crack. Dr. Rathore will tell you honestly if the tooth is worth saving.

A piece of file broke in my tooth. What now?

A separated instrument does not always cause a problem. Depending on its position it can be removed, bypassed or sealed in place. This needs careful assessment on an X-ray.
